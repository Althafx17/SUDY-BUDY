### 📐 Area 1: Polymer Molecular Weights & Polydispersity Index
*Statistical averages and molecular weight distribution index.*

#### 🔹 Number-Average Molecular Weight ($\bar{M}_n$)

$$\bar{M}_n = \frac{\sum N_i M_i}{\sum N_i}$$

> **Key Notes & Application**: Determined by colligative property methods (osmometry, ebulliometry).

#### 🔹 Weight-Average Molecular Weight ($\bar{M}_w$)

$$\bar{M}_w = \frac{\sum N_i M_i^2}{\sum N_i M_i}$$

> **Key Notes & Application**: Determined by light scattering and ultracentrifugation; sensitive to high-mass chains.

#### 🔹 Polydispersity Index (PDI)

$$\text{PDI} = \frac{\bar{M}_w}{\bar{M}_n} \ge 1$$

> **Key Notes & Application**: Monodisperse polymers (proteins) have $\text{PDI} = 1$; synthetic polymers typically have $\text{PDI} > 1.5$.

#### 🔹 Degree of Polymerization (DP)

$$\text{DP} = \frac{\bar{M}_n}{M_0} \quad (M_0 = \text{monomer repeating unit mass})$$

> **Key Notes & Application**: Indicates the average number of repeating monomer units in polymer chains.

### 📐 Area 2: Nanomaterial Surface-to-Volume Ratio
*Scaling law equations showing nanoscale property enhancements.*

#### 🔹 Spherical Nanoparticle Surface-to-Volume Ratio

$$\frac{\text{Surface Area}}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{4\pi r^2}{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3} = \frac{3}{r} = \frac{6}{d}$$

> **Key Notes & Application**: As particle diameter $d$ drops into the nanometer regime ($1-100\text{ nm}$), surface area increases dramatically, supercharging catalytic activity.

#### 📝 Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

##### Q1 [2025 · 7 Marks]

> **Question**: What are conducting polymers? Explain the mechanism of electrical conduction in Polyacetylene through p-doping and n-doping.

**Key Concept / Exam Tip**: Polyacetylene [-(CH=CH)-]n has alternating conjugated pi bonds. p-doping removes electrons (oxidation); n-doping adds electrons (reduction).

<summary><b>View Step-by-Step Model Solution</b></summary>

Definition of Conducting Polymers:
Conducting polymers (intrinsically conducting polymers, ICPs) are organic polymers featuring extended conjugated π-electron systems along their polymer backbone that display high electrical conductivity (approaching metallic levels) upon appropriate chemical or electrochemical doping.

Conduction Mechanism in Polyacetylene [-(CH=CH)-]n:
Pristine polyacetylene has alternating single and double bonds with a bandgap of ~1.5 eV, behaving as a semiconductor. Conductivity is elevated by 10^7 - 10^10 S/cm via doping:

1. p-Doping (Oxidative Doping):
- Process: Treatment of polyacetylene with an electron acceptor / oxidizing agent (e.g., Iodine vapor I2, AsF5, or FeCl3).
- Reaction:
  (CH)n + (3x/2) I2 -> [(CH)^(x+)]_n + x I3^-
- Mechanism: An electron is extracted from the valence π-band of the conjugated chain, creating a positive radical cation called a Polaron.
- Further oxidation removes a second electron, creating a dication called a Bipolaron.
- Under an applied electric field, these positive charge carriers hop and migrate rapidly along the conjugated backbone, producing high electrical conduction.

2. n-Doping (Reductive Doping):
- Process: Treatment of polyacetylene with an electron donor / reducing agent (e.g., molten sodium Na, lithium, or sodium naphthalide in THF).
- Reaction:
  (CH)n + x Na -> [(CH)^(x-)]_n + x Na^+
- Mechanism: Electrons are injected directly into the empty conduction π*-band of the polymer backbone.
- This forms negative radical anions (negative polarons) and dianions (negative bipolarons) which move freely along the polymer chain.

Final Answer:
Conducting polymers possess conjugated π systems. p-doping oxidizes the chain forming positive polarons/bipolarons; n-doping reduces the chain injecting electrons to form negative polarons/bipolarons.

</details>

#### 📝 Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

##### Q1 [2024 · 7 Marks]

> **Question**: Describe the synthesis of Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs) by Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD) method with a neat schematic diagram. State two important engineering applications of CNTs.

**Key Concept / Exam Tip**: Hydrocarbon gas (CH4 or C2H2) decomposes over transition metal catalyst nanoparticles (Fe, Co, Ni) at 700-1000 °C in a quartz tube furnace.

<details>
<summary><b>View Step-by-Step Model Solution</b></summary>

Principle of CVD Synthesis:
Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD) involves the catalytic thermal decomposition of a carbonaceous gas precursor over transition metal catalyst nanoparticles (Fe, Co, Ni) supported on a substrate inside a tube furnace at elevated temperatures.

Apparatus & Setup:
- Horizontal quartz tube placed inside a high-temperature electric furnace (700 °C - 1000 °C).
- Ceramic boat carrying catalyst nanoparticles deposited on alumina or silica substrate.
- Gas delivery system supplying precursor hydrocarbon (CH4, C2H2, or CO) mixed with carrier/reducing gas (Ar, H2).

Step-by-Step Procedure:
1. The substrate with transition metal nanoparticles is loaded into the quartz reactor.
2. The system is purged with inert argon gas and heated to reaction temperature (~800 °C).
3. Hydrocarbon gas (e.g., ethylene C2H4 or methane CH4) is introduced.
4. Catalytic Pyrolysis: Hydrocarbon decomposes on the catalytic metal nanoparticle surfaces:
   CH4(g) --[Ni / 800 °C]--> C(s) + 2 H2(g)
5. Dissolution & Precipitation: Carbon atoms dissolve into the molten metal nanoparticle until reaching saturation, then precipitate out in cylindrical graphitic sheets, forming seamless tubular CNTs.
6. Cooling: Reactor is cooled under inert gas to collect high-purity single-walled (SWCNTs) or multi-walled (MWCNTs) carbon nanotubes.

Engineering Applications:
1. Electronics & Energy Storage: Anode materials in high-capacity lithium-ion batteries and field-effect transistors due to ballistic electron conduction.
2. Structural Nanocomposites: Reinforcing agent in aerospace composite materials and lightweight athletic equipment due to exceptional tensile strength (~100 GPa).

</details>
